Yes. But after raising the bar, I would completely abandon the idea of ​​​​"H.266 plus a neural network filter." It's still evolution.

We need another standardization object: not a frame codec, but a dynamic visual scene compiler. The working title is **SceneLith**, the internal name of the architecture is **MOSAIC: Memory-Oriented Scalable Asymmetric Integer Codec**.

Main formula:

\[
Video(t)=Render(WorldState_t,\ Trajectories_t)+TruthInnovation_t+OptionalPerceptualDetail_t
\]

That is, transmit not just another picture, but changes in the world already known to the decoder.

Just a fair disclaimer: it is impossible to make an open standard that competitors will literally never catch up with. The decoder specification will by definition be available to everyone. A realistic and very strong goal sounds like this:

> Any manufacturer is capable of implementing a decoder, but for another 3-5 years no one will be able to encode as well.

The advantage must live in a monstrously strong non-standard encoder compiler, data, quality assessment models, content optimization and hardware stack.

## Why H.266 and AV1 are already hitting the ceiling

I assume that by VP1 you meant AV1. Moreover, now you need to focus on AV2: the final specification AV2 1.0 was published on May 28, 2026, and the pre-final version showed approximately 30–34% BD-rate gain relative to AV1. Therefore, “strongly overtaking AV1” is no longer enough - you need to compare with the best of AV2, VVC and modern learned codecs. [AV2 1.0](https://av2.aomedia.org/), [AV2 v13 rating](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15800).

AV1 and VVC are outstanding, but still hybrid block codecs: partitioning tree, local predictions, motion vectors, transforms, quantization, context entropy coding and filters. [VVC review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10801), [AV1 review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06091).

Their fundamental limitations:

- They encode mostly rectangular areas of frames, rather than stable objects and surfaces. A house, a face or a logo often has to be re-transmitted after a long disappearance.
- Motion compensation describes the connection of frames, but does not build long-term memory of the world.
- Increased efficiency is achieved by expanding the menu of modes. The encoder goes through a huge number of combinations, and the decoder receives a branching graph with irregular memory accesses.
- CABAC and other adaptive entropy mechanisms contain sequential dependencies that are poorly loaded on the GPU.
- Recursive blocks, intra-dependencies on the top and left border, variable transforms and filters with halo create divergence and synchronization.
- Random access, loss resistance, delay and maximum compression conflict with each other.- The format produces one “true” reconstruction and practically does not separate measurable reliability and plausible generation.

This doesn't mean that AV1/VVC are bad ASICs. On the contrary, excellent specialized blocks are made for them. But their structure fits much worse on modern tensor/SIMD GPUs than dense integer operations, warp/splat and parallel chunks.

## SceneLith: the most ambitious architecture

### 1. Encode the world, not the frames

The decoder stores a limited, deterministic state:

- 2.5D atlas of visible surfaces;
- feature planes and texture pages;
- sparse surfels/2D Gaussian splats for geometrically stable areas;
- short memory of the last frames;
- dictionary of repeating patches;
- compact one-dimensional latent tokens of long-term context;
- trajectories of the camera, objects, deformations and lighting.

If the camera sees a wall, clothing or face again after ten seconds, the codec refers to the saved surface and transmits only the change.

Goal: For a coherent scene, the bitrate should depend primarily on the amount of new information, and not directly on `width × height × FPS`. The transition from 30→120 fps with smooth motion should cost significantly less than a fourfold increase in bitrate.

For water, fire, grain, foliage and chaotic movement, the universal innovation/residual fallback remains.

### 2. Chunk-native coding

Instead of autoregressive reconstruction one frame after another, SceneLith encodes chunks of 8–16 frames with a common spatiotemporal latent representation and restores them in parallel.

Only special spine frames can change long-term state. The remaining frames read the state but do not mutate it. This gives:

- parallel decoding;
- discardable temporal layers;
- controlled delay;
- no poisoning of the entire sequence by one error;
- convenient hardware planning.

The practicality of the direction is already confirmed by DCVC-UF: the authors report 273.6 fps for 1080p and 61.9 fps for 4K on the RTX 4090, as well as -42.2% to VTM in a low-delay configuration. This is not a fair proof of a universal win over VTM-RA, but a very strong signal that multi-frame neural decoding no longer has to be slow. [DCVC-UF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04410).

### 3. Encoder as a view compiler

The heavy encoder decides how cheap it is to represent each section:

- classic wavelet/residual;
- satin and deformation;
- Gaussian/surfel primitives;
- text, vector or sprite for screen content;
- compact neural latent;
- per-scene adapter;
- exact objective residual.That is, the format becomes a visual virtual machine with a small set of hardware-implemented commands. Encoder can use a huge world model, segmentation, depth estimation and multi-minute RDO search - the decoder does not need this.

### 4. Two independent quality contracts

This is probably the most important difference.

**Fidelity/Truth Core**

- determined;
- bit-exact on different devices;
- only it participates in temporal prediction and changes memory;
- saves geometry, text, faces, identity, measured details;
- can get lossless/objective enhancement layer.

**Perceptual Shell**

- optional one-step diffusion/rectified-flow renderer;
- restores textures, grain and micro-details at extremely low bitrates;
- never becomes reference;
- each synthesized region has a provenance/uncertainty mask;
- can be completely disabled in medical, forensic, scientific and archival modes.

Single-step generative codecs already report more than 2x gains in perceptual metrics, but this does not equate to preserving the original pixels. [Single-step diffusion video coding](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Xue_Single-step_Diffusion-based_Video_Coding_with_Semantic-Temporal_Guidance_CVPR_2026_paper.html).

The universal fair “10x with the same quality” is now implausible. 5–10× are only possible for limited content or when untransmitted details are allowed to be plausibly synthesized.

### 5. Standard integer tensor/render ISA

An arbitrary loaded neural network cannot be allowed in the Main profile. Otherwise there will be neither security nor a stable ASIC.

Valid set of operations:

- INT8/INT4 weights;
- INT16 activation, INT32 accumulation;
- grouped/depthwise/1×1/3×3 convolution;
- lifting/wavelet;
- integer warp, mesh deformation and splat;
- add, concat, clamp, LUT activation;
- pixel shuffle;
- lattice/vector quantization;
- fixed tensor shapes and memory limits.

Full-resolution attention, float-dependent reconstruction, dynamic loops and a mandatory diffusion model are prohibited. DCVC-RT has already demonstrated a bit-consistent integer neural codec with real-time decoding and a claimed gain of about 21% to VTM. [DCVC-RT](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Jia_Towards_Practical_Real-Time_Neural_Video_Compression_CVPR_2025_paper.html).

### 6. New quantization and entropy layer

Instead of rounding each latent independently:

- trained lattice vector quantization in small groups;
- progressive residual VQ;
- factorized/non-autoregressive hyperprior;
- 16–64 independent interleaved RANS lines;
- offset table for independent access to tiles/chunks.Trainable lattice quantizers already show up to 22.6% gain relative to scalar quantization with a simple entropy model and smaller but useful gains with strong context. [OLVQ](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c09916f6f0c428a97a09a53648e5002e-Paper-Conference.pdf).

Relative-entropy coding and bits-back are theoretically attractive: with a general prior, you can transfer the index of the reproduced sample for approximately the price of KL. But the computational cost of the general case remains dangerously high. Therefore, REC should only be applied to small low-KL adapters and perceptual microblocks, not in the Main entropy loop. [Practice REC](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OuQYWNuNxm).

### 7. Loss-native stream

Base latent is divided into several independently decoded descriptions. Memory and base layer receive enhanced FEC; enhancement can be lost.

After the error:

- generative concealment does not change the long-term condition;
- hash detects desynchronization;
- the next spine packet repairs the state;
- checkpoints limit the spread of errors;
- each tile has a CRC and independent entropy streams.

This is much better than the “one damaged reference - the entire GOP is damaged” model.

## What to take from the latest research

In Main v1, practically implemented in 1–3 years:

- multi-frame chunk coding;
- bounded 2.5D scene memory;
- integer learned transforms;
- lattice/finite scalar quantization;
- multi-lane rANS;
- time-continuous motion splines;
- deterministic checkpoints;
- separate non-reference perceptual shell.

In optional extensions:

- one-dimensional adaptive latent tokens and long-term memory - GVC1D shows very large gains in LPIPS/DISTS, but this is a comparison with previous perceptual codecs, and not proof of superiority over AV2 in fidelity. [GVC1D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15302);
- per-scene INR/LoRA - useful for long VOD, talking heads and recurring series; some works are already bypassing VTM-RA, but encoding speed and instability between datasets are still preventing a universal profile. [GIViC](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Gao_GIViC_Generative_Implicit_Video_Compression_ICCV_2025_paper.html);
- one-step diffusion - only as display shell;
- Gaussian splats - as a regional mode, not the only representation;
- REC/shared-randomness - only for limited adapters.

Do not include in the normative decoder yet:

- full-fledged video world model;
- multi-step DiT;
- arbitrary downloadable graphs;
- full-resolution transformer;
- autoregressive entropy for each pixel;
- generative reconstruction as temporal reference;
- semantic “description of the scene in words” instead of a reliable base layer.

## Measurable moonshot goals

These are goals, not promised results.

**After 12 months**

- bit-exact 1080p60 GPU decoder;- −20…−25% BD-rate to VTM-RA on a closed set of tests;
- scene memory separately should reduce the overall bitrate by at least 10% in scenes with occlusions and camera returns;
- checkpoint/addressing overhead is less than 8%.

**After 24 months**

- 4K60 on desktop GPU;
- −35% to VTM-RA;
- minimum −10% to the strongest open learned-anchor of the same delay class;
- perceptual shell: at least 2× in bitrate with equal blind MOS;
- separate OCR, face identity, geometry and temporal-flicker gates.

**After 36 months - stretch**

- −40…−45% to VTM-RA;
- at least −15% to the then modern reproducible learned codec;
- no major content class is worse than anchor by more than 5%;
- 2–3× perceptual reduction with equal blind MOS;
- 4–10× only in specialized VOD/UI/talking-head modes;
- Main High ≤5 kMAC per output pixel, Low Compute ≤1.5 kMAC;
- baseline weights ≤32 MB, state ≤64 MB;
- random access 0.25–0.5 seconds;
- recovery after state loss ≤250 ms.

If after 18 months there is not at least a 25% fair universal gain over the contemporaneous anchor, the new standard is not justified. Then you need to turn the development into a separate tool for AV2/VVC or change the central hypothesis.

## How to create a multi-year lead

Not a secret decoder. It will kill standardization.

The separation creates the aggregate:

1. Huge encoder-only world extractor and representation router.
2. Multi-pass program/RDO search:
   \[
   J=R+\lambda D+\mu Compute+\nu Memory+\rho SeekPenalty+\sigma LossRisk
   \]
3. Closed or licensed data corpus with subjective MOS, OCR, identity, flicker and geometry labels.
4. Per-title and per-scene adaptation.
5. Accumulation of encoder solutions: each new directory improves the routing model.
6. Optimized CUDA/Vulkan kernels, FPGA and reference RTL.
7. Conformance corpus, fuzzing, streaming SDK and browser integration.
8. Narrow patents for state/checkpoint semantics, dual-contract reference rules and compute-scalable syntax - with a royalty-free or acceptable FRAND license for the standard.

Strategy formula:

> Open and simple decoder; extremely complex, constantly self-improving encoder.

## Real deadlines when working with me around the clock

Codex is able to constantly write code, tests, kernels, documentation, run experiments and analyze the results. But 24/7 does not translate into 24x speedup: model training, subjective tests, research, patent analysis, consensus and chip production remain sequential processes.

Realistic:

- 10–16 weeks - falsification prototype without a large trained model;
- 6–12 months - a real working PoC with its own bitstream;- 18–36 months - convincing alpha version;
- 3–5 years – competitive niche codec with a team of 8–15 strong specialists;
- 4–7 years - technology level of a serious proposal to the standard;
- 8–12 years, sometimes up to 15 - standard, conformance ecosystem and the first mass-produced hardware decoders.

Alone with me it is possible to create a PoC and a strong research implementation. For universal codec, standard and silicon, specialists in compression research, GPU, ASIC/RTL, subjective testing, patents and standardization will inevitably be needed.

And the window has already opened: JVET issued a call for proposals for beyond VVC technology in July 2026; the main package is expected on October 26, 2026, the assessment is in January 2027, the standard benchmark is October 2029. It is no longer realistic to prepare a full-fledged codec proposal for this window from scratch. Individual strong tools, publications, and evidence can be prepared for subsequent integrations or the next cycle. [JVET Official Page](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-T/studygroups/2025-2028/21/video/Pages/jvet.aspx), [Call for Proposals](https://www.itu.int/md/T25-SG21-260706-TD-PLEN-0348/en).
